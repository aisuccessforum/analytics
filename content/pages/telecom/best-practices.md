## Network investment

Leading operators sequence capital spend around demand density rather than blanket coverage: dense urban 5G first, where usage per site justifies the cost, with rural coverage often achieved through infrastructure sharing or government-subsidized programs rather than solo build-out.

Infrastructure sharing (co-locating towers, sometimes even sharing RAN equipment with a competitor) has become standard practice in mature markets. It cuts capex meaningfully with minimal competitive downside, since coverage is table stakes rather than a differentiator.

## Pricing and retention

The operators with the lowest churn rarely compete primarily on price. They compete on bundling, mobile, broadband, and content packaged together, because a bundled customer has more reasons to stay and a higher switching cost.

Proactive retention (identifying churn risk before a customer calls to cancel, based on usage pattern changes or competitor offer timing) consistently outperforms reactive "win-back" offers made after a cancellation request.

## Cost structure

Best-in-class operators treat network operations as a target for automation early. AI-driven network optimization and predictive maintenance reduce opex meaningfully at scale, though the payback period is long enough that this is a multi-year commitment, not a quick win.

## Common mistakes

- Over-investing in spectrum at auction relative to realistic subscriber monetization, a mistake several major operators have made and then spent years working off the resulting debt
- Under-pricing enterprise/private network deals to win logos, which sets a bad precedent for the entire enterprise segment
- Treating 5G as a consumer speed story rather than an enterprise/IoT platform story, which undersells its actual commercial case
