## Core terms

**ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)**: Monthly revenue divided by the subscriber base. The single number most telecom earnings calls open with, because it tells you whether an operator is growing by adding users or by extracting more from existing ones.

**Churn rate**: The share of subscribers who leave in a given period. Postpaid churn below 1.5% a month is considered healthy in most mature markets; prepaid churn runs far higher and is measured differently.

**MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator)**: A company that sells mobile service without owning spectrum or towers, leasing network capacity from a host operator instead. Low capex, thin margins, brand-driven.

**Spectrum**: The licensed radio frequency bands operators use to carry traffic. Acquired at auction, it is usually an operator's single largest fixed asset and the main barrier to new entrants.

**RAN (Radio Access Network)**: The towers, antennas, and base stations connecting devices to the core network. The most capital-intensive layer of the network, and the current battleground for Open RAN standardization.

**Core network**: The backend that handles authentication, routing, and switching once traffic leaves the radio layer. Increasingly virtualized and cloud-hosted rather than run on dedicated hardware.

## Generational shorthand

- **2G/3G**: Legacy voice and low-speed data, largely being sunset in developed markets
- **4G/LTE**: The current workhorse for mobile broadband
- **5G**: Higher throughput and lower latency; commercially significant less for consumer speed and more for enterprise/IoT use cases (private networks, network slicing)
- **FWA (Fixed Wireless Access)**: Using mobile spectrum to deliver home broadband, competing directly with fiber and cable

## Business model terms

**Postpaid vs. prepaid**: Contract-billed subscribers versus pay-as-you-go. Postpaid customers carry higher ARPU and lower churn, which is why operators fight to migrate prepaid users upward.

**Tower company (TowerCo)**: An entity that owns physical tower infrastructure and leases space to multiple operators. Many operators have sold towers to TowerCos to free up balance sheet capital.
