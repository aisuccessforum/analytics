## Acquisition and funnel terms

**Gross adds**: New subscribers acquired in a period, before subtracting churn. Marketing teams are usually measured on gross adds; the business as a whole cares more about net adds (gross adds minus churn).

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: Total sales and marketing spend divided by new subscribers acquired. In telecom, CAC is often inflated by device subsidies, which is why operators track "CAC excluding device subsidy" separately to see the pure marketing efficiency number.

**Conversion rate**: The share of prospects (store visits, website leads, call center inquiries) who become paying subscribers. Retail conversion rate is a closely watched metric for operators with large physical store footprints.

**Upsell / cross-sell**: Moving an existing customer to a higher-value plan (upsell) or selling them an additional product like home broadband or a streaming bundle (cross-sell). Both are cheaper than acquiring a new customer and are central to telecom marketing strategy in saturated markets.

## Segmentation and targeting

**Postpaid vs. prepaid targeting**: Marketing strategy differs sharply between these segments: postpaid campaigns emphasize brand, network quality, and bundled value; prepaid campaigns emphasize price and flexibility, since prepaid customers are far more price-sensitive and switch more easily.

**ARPU-based segmentation**: Customers are frequently grouped by how much revenue they generate, since a retention campaign that makes sense for a low-ARPU prepaid user rarely makes financial sense for a high-ARPU enterprise account.

**Look-alike audiences**: In digital campaign targeting, using the profile of existing high-value customers to find new prospects who resemble them, a standard practice borrowed from broader digital marketing and now core to telecom acquisition campaigns.

## Retention and loyalty terms

**Win-back campaign**: A targeted offer aimed at customers who have already churned, trying to bring them back, typically far less effective and more expensive than proactive retention before they leave.

**Save desk / save team**: The customer service function specifically trained to retain a customer who has called in to cancel, usually empowered to offer discounts or upgrades unavailable through normal channels.

**NPS (Net Promoter Score)**: How likely a customer is to recommend the operator, on a -100 to +100 scale. Widely tracked in telecom as a leading indicator for both churn and word-of-mouth acquisition.

## Sales channel terms

**Direct channel**: Sales through the operator's own stores, website, or call centers.

**Indirect channel**: Sales through third-party retailers, big-box stores, or authorized dealers. Indirect channels usually reach more customers at lower fixed cost but with less control over the sales experience and typically lower conversion quality.

**Channel conflict**: When direct and indirect channels compete for the same customer with different pricing or incentives, a persistent management challenge in telecom go-to-market strategy.

## Bundle and pricing terms

**FMC (Fixed-Mobile Convergence)**: Bundling mobile and home broadband under a single account and often a single bill, the primary bundling strategy used to reduce churn in mature markets.

**Tiered pricing**: Structuring plans around clear value tiers (data allowance, speed, add-ons) so customers self-select into a plan that matches their usage and budget, rather than negotiating individually.

**Promotional pricing**: Time-limited discounted rates used to drive acquisition, which marketing and finance teams monitor closely since aggressive promotional pricing that never converts to full price at renewal quietly erodes ARPU over time.
