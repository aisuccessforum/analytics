## The metrics that matter to leadership

| Metric | What it measures | Why leadership watches it |
|---|---|---|
| ARPU | Revenue per subscriber per month | Signals pricing power and upsell success |
| Churn rate | % of subscribers lost per period | Direct read on customer satisfaction and competitive pressure |
| EBITDA margin | Operating profitability | Telecom is capital-heavy; margin discipline determines free cash flow |
| CAPEX-to-revenue ratio | Network investment as % of revenue | Shows where an operator is in its network build-out cycle |
| Net subscriber additions | New minus lost subscribers | Growth signal, especially in saturated markets |
| Data usage per subscriber (GB/month) | Consumption trend | Leading indicator of future network investment needs |
| Network availability / uptime | % of time network meets SLA | Regulatory and brand-risk metric |

## Reading these together, not in isolation

A rising ARPU alongside rising churn usually means an operator is pushing price increases that customers are starting to resist. A falling CAPEX-to-revenue ratio after several years of heavy 5G spend is normal and expected — it signals the build-out phase ending and free cash flow beginning to recover.

## Metrics unique to specific segments

**Enterprise/B2B:** Contract value, SLA compliance rate, private network deployments.

**IoT/M2M:** Connected devices per subscriber account, average revenue per connection (much lower than consumer ARPU, but at far higher volume).

**Tower/infrastructure:** Tenancy ratio — the number of operators sharing a single tower. Higher tenancy ratios mean better returns on infrastructure investment.
