## People analytics

The shift from static headcount reporting toward predictive workforce analytics — attrition risk models, internal mobility recommendations, organizational network analysis. The main barrier is rarely the technology; it's data quality and integration across a fragmented HR tech stack.

## AI in recruiting

Resume screening and candidate matching were the earliest AI applications in HR, now extending into interview scheduling automation and, more cautiously, interview assessment. Leadership should be aware this is also the most heavily regulated corner of HR tech — several jurisdictions now require bias audits for AI-assisted hiring tools.

## Skills-based talent management

A structural shift away from job-title-based career frameworks toward skills-based ones, where roles, pay, and career paths are organized around verified skills rather than tenure or title. Still early in enterprise adoption but increasingly discussed at the board level as a response to skills gaps moving faster than job architectures can be redesigned.

## Employee experience platforms

Consolidating the fragmented set of tools an employee touches — onboarding, benefits, internal communications, recognition — into a single experience layer, distinct from the underlying HRIS system of record.

## HR chatbots and self-service

Reducing routine HR-service-desk volume (policy questions, leave requests, benefits queries) through conversational interfaces, freeing HR business partners for higher-judgment work.
