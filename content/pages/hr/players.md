## HRIS / Core HR platforms

The system of record for employee data — companies like Workday and SAP SuccessFactors anchor most enterprise HR tech stacks, with the HRIS choice often constraining or enabling everything else a people function can do.

## ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems)

Platforms that manage the recruiting pipeline from job posting through offer. Increasingly bundled with sourcing and assessment tools rather than sold as a standalone system.

## Talent intelligence and people analytics vendors

A newer category focused specifically on workforce data — skills mapping, attrition prediction, and organizational network analysis — sold as an analytics layer on top of the core HRIS rather than a replacement for it.

## Benefits and payroll providers

Handle the compliance-heavy, jurisdiction-specific work of paying people correctly and administering benefits. Global companies often run multiple regional providers rather than one global platform, because payroll compliance requirements vary too much by country for a single vendor to cover well everywhere.

## L&D (Learning & Development) platforms

Deliver and track training and skill-building. The category has shifted from compliance-training delivery toward skills-based career pathing tools that tie directly into internal mobility.

## Executive search and staffing firms

External partners for senior hiring and contingent workforce needs — a lever companies use to flex hiring capacity without permanently scaling internal recruiting teams.
