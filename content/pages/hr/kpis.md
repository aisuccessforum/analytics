## The metrics leadership actually tracks

| Metric | What it measures | Why leadership watches it |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary attrition rate | % of employees who choose to leave, annualized | Direct signal of engagement and competitiveness of pay/culture |
| Regrettable attrition | % of departures the company would have preferred to prevent | More useful than raw attrition — distinguishes healthy turnover from talent loss |
| Time to fill | Days from role opening to offer accepted | Recruiting capacity and market competitiveness for the role |
| Offer acceptance rate | % of offers extended that are accepted | Signals whether comp/brand is competitive at the offer stage |
| eNPS | Likelihood employees recommend the company | Leading indicator for attrition and employer brand |
| Internal mobility rate | % of roles filled internally | Career development health; correlates strongly with retention |
| Span of control | Direct reports per manager | Organizational efficiency and cost structure |
| Diversity representation at each level | Composition of workforce by level/function | Increasingly tied to both talent pipeline health and, in many markets, regulatory reporting |

## Reading these together

High offer acceptance with high early attrition (employees leaving within the first 6–12 months) usually points to a mismatch between what was sold in the hiring process and the actual role — a recruiting problem, not a retention problem. High regrettable attrition concentrated in one function or one manager's team is an organizational signal worth investigating before it shows up in the aggregate number.

## A note on benchmarking

HR metrics vary enormously by industry and geography — a "healthy" attrition rate in retail looks nothing like one in enterprise software. Any KPI here should be benchmarked against sector and region, not read against a single universal target.
