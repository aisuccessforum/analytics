## Core terms

**Attrition rate** — The percentage of employees who leave over a period, typically annualized. Voluntary attrition (employees who choose to leave) and involuntary attrition (terminations, layoffs) are tracked separately because they signal very different things.

**Time to fill** — Days between a role opening and an offer being accepted. A recruiting efficiency metric, distinct from time to hire, which measures from candidate application to offer.

**Employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS)** — How likely employees are to recommend the company as a place to work, on the same -100 to +100 scale as customer NPS.

**Total Rewards** — The full value an employee receives: base pay, bonus, equity, benefits, and increasingly, flexibility and development opportunities. Leadership increasingly evaluates compensation strategy through this wider lens rather than base salary alone.

**Span of control** — The number of direct reports a manager has. A key lever in organizational design; too wide and managers can't coach effectively, too narrow and the org becomes expensive and slow.

**HRBP (HR Business Partner)** — An HR professional embedded with a specific business unit, translating business strategy into people strategy rather than sitting in a centralized HR function.

## Talent economics

**Cost per hire** — Total recruiting cost divided by number of hires, including agency fees, advertising, and internal recruiter time.

**Quality of hire** — A composite measure of how well new hires perform, typically assessed through performance ratings and retention at the 12-month mark. Notoriously hard to standardize, which is why so few companies track it well.

**Internal mobility rate** — The share of open roles filled by existing employees rather than external hires. A rising internal mobility rate is one of the most reliable indicators of a healthy career-development culture.

**Skills gap** — The difference between the skills an organization currently has and the skills it needs, usually surfaced through workforce planning exercises tied to business strategy.
