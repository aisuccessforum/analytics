## Hiring

Structured interviews — a consistent set of questions and scoring rubrics across candidates for a given role — consistently outperform unstructured conversational interviews at predicting on-the-job performance, yet remain under-adopted because they take more upfront design work.

The strongest talent functions treat hiring managers, not recruiters, as accountable for hire quality. Recruiting's job is pipeline and process; the hiring manager's job is the decision.

## Retention

Stay interviews — structured conversations with current high performers about what would make them leave — surface retention risk earlier and more specifically than exit interviews, which arrive after the decision is already made.

Compensation reviews tied only to an annual cycle create a lag that competitors can exploit. Organizations with the lowest regrettable attrition typically run more frequent, targeted market-rate checks for flight-risk roles rather than waiting for the annual cycle.

## Organizational design

Span of control should be a deliberate design choice tied to the nature of the work, not an accident of hiring history. Roles requiring heavy coaching (early-career, high-complexity) warrant narrower spans; more autonomous, senior populations can support wider ones.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring recruiter performance purely on time-to-fill, which incentivizes speed over candidate quality
- Running engagement surveys without a visible action plan afterward, which reliably depresses scores in the next cycle
- Treating internal mobility as a retention nice-to-have rather than a structured program with the same rigor as external recruiting
